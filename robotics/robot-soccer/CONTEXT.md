# 📚 Context: Teaching a bipedal robot to play soccer

Skills were learned in simulation with heavy dynamics randomization and perturbations, plus high-frequency control, which is what made the zero-shot jump to real robots hold up. Caveats: the robots are small and low-cost, the game is a simplified 1v1, and perception is largely proprioceptive rather than full vision. It is a companion to the table-tennis robot entry in this field.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
Whole-body agile control of a bipedal robot is hard, and transferring dynamic, contact-rich skills from simulation to the real world is a long-standing challenge. This showed learned full-body control with genuine robustness.

# Honor's "Lightning" wins the Beijing humanoid half-marathon in 50:26

**Field:** 🤖 Robotics  ·  **When:** April 2026 · Beijing E-Town Humanoid Robot Half-Marathon

> A year after the first humanoid half-marathon, where the winner took 2 hours 40 minutes and only 6 of 21 robots finished, a phone maker's robot covered the same 21.1 km in 50 minutes 26 seconds, with battery swaps, a fall near the end, and handlers to pick it up.

## What happened
On April 19, 2026, the second Beijing E-Town Humanoid Robot Half-Marathon ran 21.0975 km from Kechuang 17th Street to Nanhaizi Park in Beijing's economic-technological development zone, alongside roughly 12,000 human runners. According to the Beijing municipal government's English-language report, more than 100 teams entered, robots started at 30-second intervals, the course included more than ten types of terrain, and judges in golf carts monitored rule violations and battery swaps in real time. Wikipedia's summary of the rules adds that up to three battery changes were permitted. Robots competed in two categories, fully autonomous and remotely controlled; remote-controlled finishing times were multiplied by a 1.2 weighting coefficient.

The winner was **"Lightning"** (Shandian), built by the Chinese smartphone maker Honor and entered by Team Monkey King (Qitian Dasheng), in **50 minutes 26 seconds**. Honor robots took the next two places as well, at 50:56 and 53:01. Smithsonian reports that a remotely controlled robot technically crossed the line first in 48:19, but the self-navigating Lightning was crowned champion under the weighted rules. NBC News quotes Honor's team captain, Ma Huaze, on the run: "I felt very nervous. The biggest challenge was having the courage to perform."

The time is faster than the men's human half-marathon world record, 57:20, set by Jacob Kiplimo in Lisbon on March 8, 2026 (subject to ratification at the time of the race). The fastest human in the same Beijing race ran 1:07:47.

The 2025 edition, on April 19, 2025, was won by Tiangong Ultra in about 2 hours 40 minutes; it was remotely controlled, 21 robots started and 6 finished, and Al Jazeera's report describes one robot collapsing at the gun and another running into a barrier and taking its handler down with it. In 2026, according to organisers quoted by Scientific American, **38%** of entries ran autonomously (NBC News rounds this to "nearly 40%"); the rest were piloted remotely.

## Why it matters
The half-marathon is a crude but honest benchmark: 21 km of real road, repeated once a year with the same rules, and a stopwatch. The 2025 to 2026 change (2:40 to 50:26 for the winner, 6 of 21 finishers to a field of over 100 teams with a podium sweep, zero autonomous entries to 38%) is a concrete measure of how quickly learned locomotion for full-size humanoids improved in twelve months, and one that anyone could watch.

The result should be read narrowly. Lightning was engineered for this race (Scientific American describes long legs modelled on elite runners and a liquid-cooling loop borrowed from Honor's phones), ran on a mapped, closed course, swapped batteries, and crashed into a barricade in the final stretch, where handlers set it back on its feet. The roboticists quoted in coverage were blunt: Rodney Brooks told Scientific American there is "nothing useful that you could use in any application because it shows no safety at all," and Alan Fern said the harder question is how the capability translates into productivity.

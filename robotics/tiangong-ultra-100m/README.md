# Tiangong Ultra runs 100 m in 8.64 seconds at the World Humanoid Robot Games

**Field:** 🤖 Robotics  ·  **When:** August 2026 · 2nd World Humanoid Robot Games, Beijing

> A 75 kg humanoid sprinted 100 metres in 8.64 seconds, cutting its own time from 21.50 s a year earlier. The number beats Usain Bolt's 9.58 s on paper, and the comparison is meaningless in every way that matters.

## What happened
The second World Humanoid Robot Games ran from August 22 to 26, 2026 at Beijing's National Speed Skating Oval, with **666 teams** and **2,056 robots** competing across 51 events (CGTN puts the number of individual contests at 1,301). In the large-size robot 100 m, **Tiangong Ultra**, built by the Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Centre (which brands itself X-Humanoid; its shareholders include Baidu, Xiaomi's robotics unit and state-owned enterprises), won the final in **8.64 seconds**, a Games record.

The progression over the week was steep. Tiangong Ultra ran 9.39 s on the opening day of competition (August 23), 8.86 s in a semifinal on August 25, and 8.64 s in the final that closed the Games. Honor's "Lightning" robot, the winner of April's humanoid half-marathon, ran 9.47 s in the opening round. At the inaugural Games in 2025, Tiangong's winning 100 m time had been **21.50 s**. Other track marks fell by similar margins: the 400 m went from 1:28.03 to 38.15 s and the 1,500 m from 6:34.40 to 2:21.64.

The robot cannot yet stop itself. According to Al Jazeera's report on the 8.86 s semifinal, Tiangong Ultra decelerated by running into a thick mat past the finish line, collapsed on impact, and a small fire appeared in its torso. Reuters, reporting later on the team, describes the standard set-up as "a crash mat sat beyond a short run-off area" that robots slam into after each race. Reuters also reports the robot weighs 75 kg and has a top speed above 17 m/s, and that its running is driven by a trained motion-control system that adjusts joints and balance continuously "without an operator directing every step."

## Why it matters
Two years ago a humanoid running at all, on a track, without a tether, was a demo. Cutting a 100 m time from 21.50 s to 8.64 s in twelve months is a concrete, timed, repeated-in-competition measure of how fast learned locomotion controllers for full-size humanoids have improved, and it happened in a public event with hundreds of competing teams rather than a single lab video.

The Bolt comparison, which most coverage led with, is not a comparison. Bolt started from blocks with a 0.146 s reaction time; the robot started upright and, according to Reuters' sports-science sources, waited nearly a second before moving. Humans decelerate; the robot ran into a wall. Humans race under World Athletics rules on a surface and in shoes that are regulated; the robot ran under rules written for robots. The Beijing Humanoid Robot Innovation Centre's own stated goal, per Reuters, is to turn "high performance into high reliability and high usability" for inspection and rescue work, which is a different problem from a straight sprint.

# AgiBot A2 walks 106 km from Suzhou to Shanghai, a Guinness World Record

**Field:** 🤖 Robotics  ·  **When:** November 2025 · Guinness World Records (feature published February 2026)

> A production humanoid walked 106.286 km of public roads from Suzhou's Jinji Lake to the Bund in Shanghai over 56 hours, swapping batteries 15 times, and Guinness certified it as the longest journey ever walked by a humanoid robot. Guinness states it navigated on its own; the company organised the attempt.

## What happened
Between the evening of November 10 and November 13, 2025, an **AgiBot A2** humanoid built by Agibot Innovation (Shanghai) Technology Co., Ltd. walked from Jinji Lake in Suzhou, Jiangsu province, to the Bund on the Shanghai waterfront. Guinness World Records certified the distance at **106.286 km** (66.04 miles) and the elapsed time at **56 hours 7 minutes 49 seconds**, awarding the title "Longest journey walked by a humanoid robot." Guinness published its feature on the record on February 2, 2026.

Per the Guinness account, the robot "was not guided by a human in any way and was not being controlled remotely," using its own onboard perception to sense and navigate. The route included coastlines, city-centre streets and busy roads; Interesting Engineering, summarising the company's materials, lists asphalt, tiled pavements, bridges, tactile paving, slopes and stretches with little night lighting, and says navigation used dual GPS modules with onboard lidar and infrared depth cameras. AgiBot's own account says the robot followed traffic rules and handled tiled roads, ramps and crowds. It was never powered off: hot-swappable batteries were replaced **15 times** during the walk, according to Guinness. The robot paused for 7 minutes to avoid heavy rain, and the human support team took one 17-hour rest during the attempt. Preparation included hundreds of hours of testing and a 24-hour continuous autonomous walk on August 17, 2025 in temperatures approaching 40°C.

The company says the unit was a standard production A2 with no custom modifications, and that the only wear observed afterwards was on the rubber layer of the foot soles. heise reports the A2 at 1.69 m and 69 kg including battery with 40 degrees of freedom and a roughly 3-hour battery life for the A2 Ultra version; AgiBot says it delivered more than 1,000 A2 units in 2025.

## Why it matters
Humanoid locomotion claims are usually lab videos or short races on prepared surfaces. This is a multi-day walk on real, mixed public infrastructure, on a production robot, with the distance and time adjudicated by an outside body. The average pace is unremarkable (about 1.9 km/h over the elapsed time, faster if the support team's 17-hour rest is excluded); the point is endurance and reliability: two and a half days of continuous operation on legs, with battery swaps but no shutdown, and no reported falls in the sources.

What Guinness certifies is distance, time and, on the company's evidence, the absence of remote control. It does not certify how the navigation worked, how the route was prepared, or how close the support crew stayed. See [CONTEXT.md](./CONTEXT.md).

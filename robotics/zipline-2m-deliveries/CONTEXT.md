# 📚 Context: Zipline at 2 million autonomous deliveries

**The company.** Zipline is a South San Francisco drone-delivery company founded in March 2014 by Keller Rinaudo Cliffton, Keenan Wyrobek, Ryan Oksenhorn and William Hetzler. Its first service launched in Muhanga, Rwanda, in October 2016, flying blood and medical supplies from a distribution centre to rural clinics with the fixed-wing "P1" aircraft: a 44-pound drone with a 4-pound payload that drops packages under a paper parachute into a 5-metre zone and is recovered by a tail hook catching an arresting wire. It has since operated in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Côte d'Ivoire, Japan and the United States. The U.S. consumer business, with Walmart in the Dallas area among others, uses the "P2" aircraft, a vertical-take-off hybrid that hovers and lowers a small delivery "droid" on a tether, placing packages within about a metre.

**The autonomy.** Zipline aircraft fly pre-planned routes autonomously, with onboard sensing for detect-and-avoid and precision delivery; human operators supervise fleets rather than piloting individual aircraft. This is the operational definition of autonomy used across the industry, and it is why the mileage figure is stated as "autonomous commercial miles." The company has not published details of its perception stack.

**The numbers and their status.** Everything quantitative in this entry comes from the January 21, 2026 press release, reproduced on GlobeNewswire and summarised by The Robot Report and DroneLife. The two trade outlets added no independent verification; they relay the company's figures with some product context (P2 at Walmart, 10-minute deliveries). No regulator, auditor or academic group has published an independent count of Zipline's deliveries or miles.

**How the milestone was reached.** The first million deliveries took about seven and a half years: Zipline announced its one-millionth delivery on April 22, 2024 (two bags of IV fluid in Ghana), with more than 70 million autonomous miles flown at that point. The second million took about 21 months and roughly 55 million miles, driven by the U.S. retail rollout; the January 2026 release cites 15% week-over-week U.S. growth for seven months and new sites ramping to 100 deliveries a day in 2 days.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
Because it is the largest documented deployment of autonomous aircraft doing routine paid work, at a scale (125 million miles, 2 million deliveries, thousands of flights a day) where reliability and safety are measured by operations rather than demonstrations. The entry is about deployment scale, not a research result.

## Honest caveats
- **Company press release, unaudited.** The delivery count, mileage, item count and safety record are Zipline's own statements, issued alongside a $600 million fundraise. No third party has verified them.
- **"Saving more than 10,000 lives per year" is not repeated here as fact.** The release makes this claim without a methodology, a study, or a definition of "saved." Faster medical deliveries plausibly help patients, but turning that into a lives-per-year count would need a published analysis, and none is cited. Treat the figure as marketing.
- **"Without a serious injury" is undefined.** The release does not say what counts as serious, whether it covers ground crew, or how incidents are logged. The claim concerns injury to people, not aircraft losses or precautionary landings, which the release does not address.
- **"More than all other companies combined" is a competitive claim** with no cited data on competitors' volumes.
- **Autonomy is supervised.** Aircraft fly autonomously, but fleet operators monitor flights and can intervene. This is standard and not hidden, but "autonomous" should not be read as unsupervised.
- **The mix of deliveries is not broken out.** Medical deliveries in Africa and grocery deliveries in Texas are very different operations; the release gives one combined number.
- **No AI method is described.** This is a robotics deployment entry. Zipline has not published what perception or planning systems its aircraft use, so nothing here is reproducible from the sources.

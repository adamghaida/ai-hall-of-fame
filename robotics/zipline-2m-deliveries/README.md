# Zipline passes 2 million autonomous drone deliveries and 125 million autonomous miles

**Field:** 🤖 Robotics  ·  **When:** January 2026 · Zipline press release

> A fleet of autonomous fixed-wing and hybrid drones has now made more than 2 million commercial deliveries over more than 125 million miles, from blood bags in Rwanda to groceries in Dallas, with no serious injury reported. The figures are the company's own, published alongside a $600 million funding round.

## What happened
On January 21, 2026, Zipline announced that its aircraft had surpassed **2 million commercial deliveries**, which the company describes as more than all other companies in the sector combined, and had flown **more than 125 million autonomous commercial miles**, carrying more than **20 million items** "without a serious injury." The announcement accompanied a funding round of more than **$600 million** (investors named include Fidelity Management & Research, Baillie Gifford, Valor Equity Partners and Tiger Global) at a **$7.6 billion** valuation, and expansion to Houston and Phoenix in early 2026 with at least four more U.S. states planned for the year.

Zipline's operating statistics, as reported in the release: a delivery somewhere in the world every 30 seconds; a median flight time of 3 minutes; operations on four continents serving more than 5,000 hospitals and health facilities; U.S. delivery volume growing roughly 15% week over week for the preceding seven months; and new sites reaching 100 deliveries a day within 2 days, against 10 weeks for the company's first Dallas location. The Robot Report and DroneLife, covering the announcement, note that U.S. consumer deliveries use the newer P2 aircraft (a hovering drone that lowers packages on a tether) at Walmart locations in Texas, with deliveries in as little as 10 minutes for eligible customers.

The company began in Rwanda in October 2016 delivering blood and medical supplies by fixed-wing drone to rural clinics, and reached its first million deliveries on April 22, 2024, with about 70 million miles flown by then. The second million took roughly 21 months. The U.S. retail business is the newer and faster-growing segment.

## Why it matters
Drone delivery has been "two years away" for over a decade. Zipline's count is the first publicly stated figure at this scale for a single operator of fully autonomous aircraft doing paid deliveries beyond visual line of sight, and 125 million miles is enough flying for the safety record to mean something: at that exposure, a fleet with a serious-injury rate comparable to general aviation would have had incidents to report. The autonomy is real in the operational sense that matters: the aircraft plan, fly, avoid, and deliver without a pilot on the sticks, at a cadence of thousands of flights a day.

The numbers are unaudited company statements in a fundraising press release, and the release also claims the service is "saving more than 10,000 lives per year," a figure with no published methodology behind it that this entry does not repeat as fact. See [CONTEXT.md](./CONTEXT.md).

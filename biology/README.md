AI didn't just read the language of life — it started writing in it.

### AlphaFold — the shape of (almost) every protein

- **What happened:** AlphaFold2 predicts protein 3D structure from sequence with near-experimental accuracy, and DeepMind published structures for **~200 million proteins**, essentially the entire known protein universe.
- **When:** 2020–2021 (recognized with the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry).
- **Why it's a big deal:** Protein shape determines function, so this reshaped molecular biology, drug discovery, enzyme engineering, and disease research overnight. (Full write-up in [Chemistry](../chemistry/).)
- **Sources:** [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/science/alphafold/) · [Nobel press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/press-release/)

### RFdiffusion — designing brand-new proteins from scratch

- **What happened:** David Baker's lab at the Institute for Protein Design built **RFdiffusion**, a generative diffusion model that designs functional proteins *de novo* — including folds never seen in nature. It can create proteins up to ~600 residues, and hundreds of AI-designed proteins were experimentally validated as potential medicines, vaccines, and nanomaterials.
- **When:** 2022–2023 (*Nature*, 2023).
- **Why it's a big deal:** Diffusion models pushed lab success rates from roughly 1-in-10,000 to a few percent — a leap that turned protein design from a rare art into an engineering discipline. Baker shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for computational protein design.
- **Sources:** [Institute for Protein Design](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2022/12/a-diffusion-model-for-protein-design/) · [Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06415-8)

# 📚 Context: Pluribus — superhuman six-player poker

Two-player zero-sum poker has a well-defined optimal (Nash) strategy; multiplayer does not, which is why it was hard. Pluribus's answer was pragmatic: don't chase an unattainable equilibrium, just search a few moves ahead over plausible opponent continuations. The cheap compute footprint surprised many and underlined that algorithmic ideas, not just scale, drive breakthroughs.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
It cracked a decades-old milestone in game theory and AI — and did so cheaply, training in days on modest hardware rather than a supercomputer.

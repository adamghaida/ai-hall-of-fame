# 📚 Context: AXOL1TL in the CMS Level-1 Trigger

**The people.** AXOL1TL is a project of the **CMS Collaboration** at CERN's Large Hadron Collider, developed within the Level-1 trigger group with the fast machine learning community around **hls4ml**. Public presentations have been given by, among others, Noah Zipper (the 2023 test-crate paper), Abhijith Gandrakota (ICHEP 2024 proceedings, on behalf of CMS), Sabrina Giorgetti (Fast Machine Learning for Science 2025, Padova/INFN), Maciej Glowacki (ACAT 2025, CERN) and Natalie Bruhwiler (APS Global Physics Summit 2026, University of Colorado Boulder). The collaboration's own record of its operation is the CMS detector-performance note "Anomaly detection with AXOL1TL at the CMS Level-1 Trigger in 2024 and 2025".

**The problem.** CMS sees proton-bunch crossings every 25 ns. The Level-1 trigger, built from FPGAs and custom boards, has a fixed latency budget of about 4 µs to decide whether each crossing is read out, taking 40 MHz down to about 100 kHz before software (the High-Level Trigger) sees anything. The L1 "menu" is a list of hand-designed conditions: thresholds on the energies and multiplicities of jets, leptons, photons and missing energy, chosen to catch the signatures physicists already expect. New physics with a signature nobody wrote a rule for is discarded at this stage, permanently. Anomaly detection is the attempt to keep a budgeted fraction of events precisely because they are unlike the bulk.

**How AXOL1TL works.** The model is a variational autoencoder trained, unsupervised, on Zero Bias data (a random sample of crossings with no selection at all), so that it learns the distribution of ordinary events. The input is a fixed-length list of the L1 objects available at the Global Trigger: the (pT, η, φ) of up to 10 jets, 4 electrons/photons and 4 muons plus missing transverse energy, in the integer representation the hardware uses. Only the encoder is deployed; the anomaly score is the sum of the squared latent means over an 8-dimensional latent space, which avoids running the decoder in firmware. The network is quantised and translated to FPGA firmware through hls4ml and runs on the Global Trigger's Xilinx Virtex-7 FPGAs with a latency of about 50 ns. Five score thresholds define trigger "seeds" of different rates. Training used 2023 Zero Bias data split evenly between training and validation.

**Timeline.** A first version was integrated into the Global Trigger test crate during Run 3 in 2023, where it could be validated on live collisions without affecting data-taking. It was commissioned into the production Level-1 trigger in 2024 (the ICHEP proceedings describe commissioning during June 2024 data-taking; other CMS material refers to integration into the L1 menu in spring 2024). In 2025 an updated model was deployed with a larger rate budget and a new self-supervised feature-extraction front end. A complementary algorithm, **CICADA**, applies a convolutional autoencoder to lower-level calorimeter data in a different part of the trigger.

**What the studies show.** In the ICHEP proceedings' simulation studies, AXOL1TL at a 1 kHz rate gave a 46% efficiency gain over the rest of the L1 menu for an exotic Higgs decay to four b-quarks, and the events it collects are largely orthogonal to those selected by conventional seeds, favouring higher-multiplicity topologies such as soft unclustered energy patterns (SUEPs). Mass distributions of selected events showed no obvious sculpting artefacts, which matters for later analyses.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
This is machine learning in the one place in an experiment where a wrong decision is unrecoverable, running at 40 MHz, in production, for two running years. It changes what data exists. The engineering constraint (a neural network in about 50 ns on an FPGA inside a 4 µs pipeline, in a system that must not halt data-taking) is as much the achievement as the algorithm. It is also a clear instance of the "anomaly detection at scale" pattern seen elsewhere in this collection, for example [AnomalyMatch on the Hubble archive](../../astronomy/anomalymatch-hubble-archive/), but applied before the data are stored rather than after.

## Honest caveats
- **No discovery.** Nothing new has been found in the AXOL1TL-selected data as of the sources cited here. The result is a new data stream and a demonstrated selection capability, not a physics result.
- **Performance figures are from simulation.** The 46% efficiency gain for Higgs to four b-quarks and the SUEP sensitivity come from simulated signals in the proceedings; real-data validation so far concerns rates, stability and orthogonality to the existing menu.
- **"Anomalous" means "unlike Zero Bias," not "new physics."** The score flags events far from the bulk of ordinary collisions, which includes rare Standard Model processes, pile-up fluctuations and detector effects as well as any genuine new physics.
- **Small rate budget.** AXOL1TL seeds get a limited share of the 100 kHz L1 bandwidth, and the operating point sets how much of the tail is kept; the 2025 update increased that budget, but it remains a fraction of the trigger.
- **Dates differ by source.** The proceedings describe commissioning in June 2024 data-taking; other CMS material refers to integration into the menu in spring 2024. This entry uses June 2024 as the documented date.
- **Primary record is a collaboration note, not a refereed journal paper.** The fullest documentation is the CMS detector-performance note on the CERN Document Server (which we could not fetch directly because of the site's bot protection) plus conference proceedings and talks. The 2023 test-crate paper and the ICHEP 2024 proceedings are on arXiv.

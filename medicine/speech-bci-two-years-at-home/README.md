# Two years of a speech brain-computer interface, used at home with nobody from the lab present

**Field:** 🩺 Medicine  ·  **When:** June 2026 · Nature Medicine

> A man with ALS spoke nearly 2 million words through an AI decoder over almost two years, on his own schedule, in his own house, with no researcher in the room.

## What happened
On June 15, 2026, *Nature Medicine* published "Long-term independent use of an intracortical brain-computer interface for speech and cursor control" by Nicholas S. Card and colleagues at UC Davis, Brown University, and Mass General Brigham, working under the **BrainGate2** clinical trial.

The participant is **Casey Harrell**, 47, who has ALS. He has weakness in his arms and legs and dysarthria severe enough that his residual speech is very hard to understand. In 2023, neurosurgeon David Brandman implanted four microelectrode arrays, **256 cortical electrodes** in total, in Harrell's left precentral gyrus. Neural activity from his attempts to speak is decoded into text against a **125,000-word vocabulary**; separate decoding of attempted hand movement drives a mouse cursor.

Earlier speech-BCI results, including this system's own 2024 *NEJM* report and the separate Stanford and UCSF work covered in [the 2023 speech-neuroprosthesis entry](../speech-neuroprosthesis/), were lab results: a researcher had to be present to set the system up. This paper is about what happened after that requirement was removed. Over **nearly two years**, Harrell used the BCI for **more than 3,800 hours at home with no researchers present**. In that time he communicated **183,060 sentences totaling 1,960,163 words**, at an average of **56 words per minute**. He labeled **92%** of those sentences as decoded at least mostly correctly.

The **99% figure** that headlines much of the coverage is narrower and worth stating precisely: in formal test sessions where Harrell was asked to say words shown on a screen, attempted speech was decoded with **more than 99% word accuracy** over the 125,000-word vocabulary. That is a controlled copy task, not a measure of the free, unscripted conversation that made up the 3,800 hours. The self-rated 92% is the closer proxy for everyday use.

Using speech decoding as keyboard input and cursor decoding as mouse input, he ran his own personal computer: text messages, email, the web, and enough independent work to sustain full-time employment.

## Why it matters
Nearly every headline BCI result of the past decade, including [the handwriting BCI](../../neuroscience/handwriting-bci/) and the 2023 speech neuroprostheses, has been measured in a lab, in a session, with the people who built it standing by. The interesting variable here is not the decoding accuracy, which was already high. It is the **absence of the research team**: a decoder that stays calibrated across nearly two years of daily life, handled by a home care team, is a different kind of object than a decoder that wins a benchmark.

That is the step assistive technology has to clear to be technology rather than a study, and this is the first time an intracortical speech BCI has been documented clearing it. In July 2026, co-senior author Sergey Stavisky received the Chen Institute and Science Prize for AI Accelerated Research for this line of work.

The caveats are large and the paper does not hide them. This is **one person**. It required brain surgery and an **investigational device**, labeled by federal law as limited to investigational use and not approved for general clinical care. The hardware still runs off a cart and connects through percutaneous pedestals. What worked for Harrell does not predict what will work for anyone else, and ALS is progressive, so the long-term picture for him is genuinely uncertain.

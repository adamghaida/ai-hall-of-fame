# 📚 Context: WeatherNext Cyclones and the National Hurricane Center

**The problem.** Tropical cyclone forecasting has two halves. Track (where the storm goes) is mostly set by the large-scale steering flow, and global models have improved it steadily for decades. Intensity (how strong it gets) depends on the storm's inner core, ocean heat and small-scale processes that global models at 25 km cannot resolve, so agencies lean on regional models such as NOAA's HAFS and on statistical aids. Rapid intensification, a jump of 30 kt or more in 24 hours, is the most dangerous failure mode: it is what turns a Category 1 forecast into a Category 5 landfall. Earlier AI weather models ([GraphCast](../graphcast-weather/), [GenCast](../gencast-ensemble-weather/)) improved track but inherited the intensity underestimation of the coarse reanalysis they were trained on.

**The approach.** WeatherNext Cyclones is trained on two data sources at once: ECMWF global analyses, which teach the atmosphere, and IBTrACS best-track records of nearly 5,000 cyclones over 45 years, which teach the cyclone-specific quantities (centre position, maximum wind, wind radii) that the coarse grid cannot represent. The model produces an ensemble of global weather scenarios and, for each, a cyclone track with intensity and size, out to 15 days in 6-hour steps. It has about 180 million parameters, is a deep ensemble of four independently trained networks, and is initialised each cycle from ECMWF's operational analysis. Paper authors include Ferran Alet, Tom Andersson, Ilan Price, Stratis Markou, Andrew El-Kadi, Dominic Masters and Peter Battaglia at Google DeepMind, with collaborators at NHC, NOAA, CIRA (Colorado State University's Cooperative Institute for Research in the Atmosphere) and the UK Met Office.

**The timeline.**
- **12 June 2025.** DeepMind and Google Research launch Weather Lab, publishing live experimental cyclone predictions (50 scenarios, 15 days) and announce that NHC forecasters "are now seeing live predictions from our experimental AI models, alongside other physics-based models and observations." On 2023 and 2024 storms in the North Atlantic and East Pacific, the model's 5-day track was on average 140 km closer to the true position than ECMWF ENS, and its intensity error beat HAFS. CIRA's Kate Musgrave is quoted saying the model showed "comparable or greater skill than the best operational models for track and intensity."
- **2025 Atlantic season.** Experimental WN-C forecasts go to NHC, which uses them alongside its other guidance. For Hurricane Melissa, DeepMind says the model gave an 80% probability of a Category 5 landfall in Jamaica five days ahead while the storm was still Category 1, rising to near 100% three days out; NHC Director Michael Brennan and Jamaica Meteorological Service Principal Director Evan Thompson are quoted on how the advance warning was used. Melissa struck Jamaica in October 2025 as the strongest hurricane in the island's record.
- **30 March 2026.** NHC's 2025 verification report finds the Google DeepMind ensemble mean (GDMI) slightly better than the official forecast on Atlantic track at 12 to 72 hours and considerably better than other models in the eastern North Pacific at 48 to 120 hours. Details are in the [operational-weather entry](../ai-weather-models-operational/).
- **6 August 2026.** The Nature paper and open-source release. 5-day track error 230 km vs 370 km (ENS) and 335 km (GenCast); 3-day intensity 3.75 kt better than HAFS; better 34-kt wind radii than ENS and HAFS; rapid-intensification critical success index raised from below 0.3 to 0.5. The ensemble for the 2026 season is 1,000 members.

**The release.** The repository ships WeatherNext 2, WeatherNext Cyclones (with dated checkpoints so the 2023 and 2024 paper results can be reproduced, plus the checkpoint actually used in 2025) and a 1° "mini" variant for free Colab hardware. Code is Apache 2.0; other materials, which is where the weights sit, are CC BY 4.0.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
This is the rare AI forecasting result with the full chain: a live season at the responsible agency, an independent verification report from that agency, a peer-reviewed paper with specific error numbers against the operational baselines, and the code and weights in public. The gain is also large in a domain where gains are usually incremental and where the agency's own official forecast is the hardest benchmark.

## Honest caveats
- **Experimental guidance, not the official forecast.** Throughout 2025 WN-C was one input among many; NHC's official forecast remained a human product. Weather Lab carries the notice that its predictions "are generated by models still under development and are not official warnings." The paper's statement that forecasters had begun incorporating its guidance is DeepMind's characterisation.
- **Most numbers are DeepMind's.** The 230 km, 370 km, 3.75 kt and lead-time figures come from the authors' own evaluation in the paper. The independent numbers are NHC's, and they cover a single season with an incomplete sample (GDMI was not available for the earliest 2025 storms). See the operational-weather entry for what NHC did and did not find.
- **Comparisons are against ENS and HAFS, not against NHC's official forecast, in the paper.** ENS is the strongest global ensemble, but agency official forecasts, which blend many models and human judgement, are usually better than any single model. NHC's report is the place where the official forecast is the baseline.
- **Wind speed is bounded by the training data.** The authors note the model is trained on coarse analysis data, which limits absolute wind-speed precision, and that IBTrACS wind radii are approximate because real winds are not monotonic with distance from the centre.
- **Rapid intensification is better, not solved.** A critical success index of 0.5 means roughly half of the forecast or observed RI events are still misses or false alarms.
- **Three seasons.** The test set is 2023 to 2025. Cyclone verification is noisy year to year, and one strong Atlantic season (2025 had three Category 5 storms) is not a guarantee about the tails.
- **Scope.** The paper covers track, intensity and wind radii. Cyclone rainfall, storm surge and precise gusts, which drive much of the damage, are listed by the authors as future work.
- **A discrepancy in secondary coverage.** Some write-ups of the August 2026 release give different kilometre figures for the track comparison. The entry uses the Nature paper's numbers (230 km vs 370 km at 5 days), which are consistent with the 140 km gap DeepMind reported in June 2025.

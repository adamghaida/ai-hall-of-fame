# 📚 Context: Claude's cryptanalysis of HAWK and reduced-round AES

**The single most important thing.** No deployed cryptography was broken, weakened, or put at risk. HAWK was a candidate under evaluation, not a standard, and was never shipped in production software. The AES result attacks a 7-round variant of AES-128; real AES-128 uses 10 rounds, and the attack additionally needs 2^105 chosen plaintexts, roughly 4 x 10^31 messages, which is not a quantity anyone will ever collect. The finalized post-quantum standards (FIPS 203 ML-KEM, FIPS 204 ML-DSA, FIPS 205 SLH-DSA) are untouched. Anthropic states this plainly in the announcement: "neither of these results has a practical impact on today's computer systems; no production software will have to change as a result."

**Reduced-round cryptanalysis is the normal unit of progress.** Attacking a cipher with some of its rounds removed is how cryptographers measure a design's safety margin. AES has stood at 10 rounds precisely because attacks on 6, 7, and 8 rounds map out how much headroom remains. A better 7-round attack is a real contribution to that literature and simultaneously a non-event for anyone using AES. Both things are true.

**HAWK and the automorphism.** HAWK is a lattice signature scheme built on the module lattice isomorphism problem, submitted to NIST's call for Additional Digital Signatures and still in round 3 after about two years of expert scrutiny. Its security relies on the difficulty of recovering a secret basis transformation. In "HAWK-n Key Recovery Reduces to SVP in Dimension n/2 + 1", Zygimantas Straznickas and Stephen A. Weis describe how Claude Mythos Preview identified a second order-2 Galois automorphism in the power-of-two cyclotomic field HAWK works over, the map sending a root of unity to its negative, and built a rank-n "cocycle lattice" whose shortest vector is exactly the cocycle V_tau = B^-1 tau(B). Getting from that vector to the secret transformation takes one more step: a van Gent and Pulles kernel-sublattice descent followed by a second Ducas run. That gives an unconditional, deterministic polynomial-time reduction from HAWK-n key recovery to exact SVP in dimension n/2 + 1.

**The prior human work matters, in both directions.** At the Sixth NIST PQC Standardization Conference in 2025, Daniël M. H. van Gent and Ludo N. Pulles presented "HAWK: Having Automorphisms Weakens Key", which established that a suitable nontrivial automorphism *would* cut the problem to half rank, but noted that no such automorphism was known for HAWK's construction. So the shape of the danger was already documented by humans. What the model contributed was the missing object. That is a narrower claim than "the AI saw something no human could have," and it is also a genuine one: the gap sat open for a year with the right people looking at it.

**The consequence.** Steve Weis posted the result to the NIST pqc-forum on July 28, 2026, noting the work was found by Claude with minimal technical guidance. Daniel Apon replied within hours: "It checks out independently for me." Discussion turned quickly to whether HAWK still made sense, given that it already lacked a decisive advantage over ML-DSA-44 and that restoring the margin means doubling key sizes. The submission team withdrew HAWK from the standardization process, and NIST's round-3 page now states that the team has withdrawn it. This appears to be the first time an AI-originated cryptanalytic result has changed the outcome of a NIST standardization process.

**The AES side.** In a separate effort, Milad Nasr and Nicholas Carlini built a scaffold that let Mythos work autonomously on 7-round AES-128 for about a week and roughly a billion output tokens, hitting on the central idea three days in. The model produced an invariant fingerprint it called the "Möbius Bridge", exploiting the invert-then-affine-transform algebraic structure of the AES S-box to make the attack invariant to one of the nine key bytes that prior meet-in-the-middle attacks had to guess. That removes a factor of 256 from the guessing work and drops time complexity from about 2^99 to between 2^89.3 and 2^91.4 depending on the accounting, the source of the "200 to 800 times faster" figure. The data complexity does not improve.

**CryptanalysisBench.** Alongside the two results, Anthropic worked with academics at ETH Zurich, Tel Aviv University, and TU Berlin on a benchmark for this capability. The paper, "CryptanalysisBench: Can LLMs do Cryptanalysis?" (Lukas Fluri, Avital Shafran, Nicholas Carlini, Matthew Jagielski, Milad Nasr, Orr Dunkelman, Eyal Ronen, Florian Tramèr), assembles 191 tasks across six cryptographic primitive families from NIST competitions in three difficulty tiers, and reports that current models break 65% to 86% of tier-1 schemes, including a previously unknown key-recovery attack on SpoC AEAD and an error found in KINDI's security proof.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
A frontier model found a structural weakness in a cryptographic scheme that had survived two years of expert review, the finding was independently verified by cryptographers within hours on a public mailing list, and it directly caused a NIST post-quantum candidate to be withdrawn from standardization. The result is checkable: papers, gate-count estimates, and running code that recovers a real HAWK-256 key.

## Honest caveats
- **Nothing deployed is affected.** Repeating it because the headline version of this story is wrong. HAWK was never in production. Full AES is untouched. No user, company, or protocol needs to do anything.
- **HAWK-512 and HAWK-1024 were never practically broken.** 2^108 and 2^182 are both far beyond feasibility. HAWK was withdrawn because the *margin* collapsed and the fix would have made it uncompetitive, not because anyone could forge signatures.
- **The AES result is a research improvement, not a break.** 2^105 chosen plaintexts is the binding constraint and the new attack does not reduce it. Anthropic notes the attack would cost hundreds of millions of dollars to even attempt, on 7 of 10 rounds.
- **Humans framed the problem and did the verification.** The AES scaffold, the choice of target, the responsible-disclosure coordination with NIST, and several hundred hours of validation were all human. Reporting also indicates the model initially declined the AES task and needed repeated prompting. The discovery was fast; confirming it was not.
- **Attribution is partly a judgment call.** The automorphism angle had been flagged as promising by human researchers in 2025. How much credit belongs to the model versus the scaffolding, the problem framing, and the existing literature is a fair question, and Anthropic's own framing (a researcher acting as project manager) concedes some of it.
- **It was not the only dimension reduction on HAWK that week.** Hours after Weis's post, Hengyi Luo and coauthors replied on the same pqc-forum thread with an independent attack produced by GPT-5.6, also with minimal guidance, reducing key recovery to SVP in dimension 3n/4. Within days Guilhem Mureau and Alice Pellet-Mary reached at most 3n/4 + 1 by an unrelated trace-zero route, and Cong Ling pointed to human dimension-reduction results already in the literature (Eurocrypt 2026, ePrint 2025/1448). Anthropic's n/2 + 1 is strictly stronger than any of these, and it is the result the HAWK team cited in withdrawing, so nothing above changes. But the problem was visibly in the air, which softens the "two years of humans missed it" framing.
- **These are lab-published results, not peer-reviewed papers.** The HAWK reduction received rapid public verification on pqc-forum, which is strong. The AES paper has had less external scrutiny at time of writing. Daniel Apon's point on the forum stands: the community does not yet have agreed standards for adjudicating AI-generated cryptanalytic claims at volume.
- **Cost is not nothing.** Roughly $100,000 in API spend per result. This is not yet a technique anyone can point at an arbitrary cipher for free.

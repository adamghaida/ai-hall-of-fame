# 📚 Context: Claude Mythos Preview and Project Glasswing

**Where it came from.** Anthropic's Frontier Red Team had already reported in February 2026 that Claude Opus 4.6, with no custom scaffolding, found more than 500 high-severity zero-days in open-source projects ([Anthropic's report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/zero-days)). Mythos Preview was the next model, and the April 7 assessment was written to explain why it would not ship normally. The claim was not just more bugs but a different tier of them: autonomous exploit development, chaining two to four kernel bugs for Linux privilege escalation, chaining four browser bugs to escape renderer and OS sandboxes, and working exploits for fully patched targets. Anthropic's own comparison table has Opus 4.6 finding 150 to 175 tier-1 and about 100 tier-2 crashes on OSS-Fuzz targets with a single tier-3 result, against Mythos Preview's 595 tier-1 and tier-2 crashes and ten full control-flow hijacks.

**The named bugs, and who fixed them.** The two headline findings were both in BSD kernels and both received ordinary vendor fixes before Anthropic said anything. OpenBSD's 7.8 errata patch 025 (March 25, 2026) fixes SACK option validation and does not mention Anthropic or Claude at all; the attribution to Mythos comes from Anthropic's report, which describes the bug as a signed integer overflow in SACK block handling leading to a null-pointer dereference and remote denial of service. FreeBSD's advisory SA-26:08 (March 26, 2026) for CVE-2026-4747 does carry the credit "Nicholas Carlini using Claude, Anthropic"; the bug is a stack buffer overflow in `svc_rpc_gss_validate()` where an RPC header is rebuilt into a 128-byte stack buffer without checking the credential body fits. Anthropic's report describes a working remote exploit, and adds, with unusual candor, "We are not kernel developers, and so our understanding here may be imperfect. We are very confident in the correctness of the exploits."

**Project Glasswing.** The distribution model is the novel part. Instead of a general release, Mythos Preview went to twelve launch partners and 40+ further organizations, with $100M in credits, $4M in donations to open-source security foundations, a pledge to publicly report fixed findings within 90 days, and a stated post-preview price of $25 per million input tokens and $125 per million output tokens. Anthropic also said security professionals whose legitimate work was blocked by the model's cyber safeguards could apply to a Cyber Verification Program. By June 2026 access had reportedly widened to about 150 organizations.

**Mozilla's numbers.** Mozilla is the partner that published the most. Its May 7 engineering post (Brian Grinstead, Christian Holler, Frederik Braun) describes an agentic harness that has the model form a hypothesis about a bug, then write and run a reproducible test case to confirm it before reporting, which is what earlier LLM auditing lacked. Result: 271 previously unknown bugs fixed in Firefox 150 (180 sec-high, 80 sec-moderate, 11 sec-low), some of them 15 to 20 years old, out of a record 423 security issues resolved in April. Mozilla's CTO Bobby Holley was also the one who said, "We also haven't seen any bugs that couldn't have been found by an elite human researcher."

**The May 22 update.** Six weeks in, Anthropic reported the aggregate: over 10,000 high or critical vulnerabilities across roughly 50 partners; 23,019 findings from a scan of 1,000+ open-source projects, 6,202 of them estimated high or critical; six independent security firms assessing 1,752 sampled findings and validating 90.6% (1,587) as real, 62.4% as high or critical; 530 high or critical bugs disclosed to maintainers, 75 patched, 65 with public advisories; Cloudflare reporting about 2,000 bugs of which 400 were high or critical; a two-week average time to patch. The update names only a few specific fixes (a wolfSSL certificate-forgery bug, CVE-2026-5194, among them) because most are still inside coordinated-disclosure windows.

## Why it's in the Hall of Fame
It is the largest documented vulnerability-discovery result attributed to an AI system, and the parts that can be checked check out: an OpenBSD errata patch, a FreeBSD advisory naming the model, Mozilla's independently published count, and an external validation sample. It also changed how a frontier model was released, which is a first of its own.

## Honest caveats
- **The big numbers are self-reported, and disputed.** "Over 10,000" is what partners told Anthropic; there is no public CVE list, no CVSS distribution, and no per-partner breakdown. The Register's April 22 roundup collected the pushback: Davi Ottenheimer noted the 244-page system card's security section contains no count of zero-days; Patrick Garrity of VulnCheck estimated the real CVE count at "maybe 40. Or maybe none at all"; Horizon3.ai's Snehal Antani called it "a nothingburger" on the grounds that attackers do not need Mythos; and the analyst Devansh, reading Anthropic's primary sources, argued that the Firefox exploits "ran with the browser sandbox turned off," that the FreeBSD exploit "shows substantial human guidance, not autonomy," and that "thousands of severe vulnerabilities" extrapolates from 198 manually reviewed reports. That last point is accurate: the April projection was explicitly conditional on the 198-report review rate holding.
- **The 72.4% exploit success figure leans on two bugs.** Critics reading Anthropic's own evaluation noted that Claude Opus 4.6 found the Firefox bugs before Mythos was handed them for exploitation, that the exploit runs used a security-reduced test build, and that removing the two most-exploited (now patched) bugs drops Mythos's end-to-end success rate to under 5%. These are readings of Anthropic's report rather than independent experiments; they have not been rebutted in detail.
- **Cheap open models found the headline bug too.** On the same day as the announcement, AISLE (Stanislav Fort) fed the vulnerable FreeBSD function to eight models in single zero-shot API calls; all eight found the overflow, including a 3.6B-active-parameter model billed at $0.11 per million tokens, and a 5.1B-active open-weights model recovered the core OpenBSD SACK chain in one call. AISLE's own caveat is that this is an upper bound: the models were pointed at the function, often with hints, with no tool use or search. Finding a bug when told where to look is not the same as scanning a kernel. But it does undercut the claim that this bug class required a frontier model.
- **Most of it was unpatched.** Anthropic said over 99% of findings were unpatched on April 7. By May 22, 75 of 530 disclosed open-source bugs had patches. Thousands of validated high-severity bugs known to a vendor consortium but not to the public is a new situation, and the 90 + 45 day disclosure clock means the record will stay incomplete into late 2026.
- **The validation sample is not the whole set.** 90.6% is measured on 1,752 findings assessed by six firms Anthropic engaged and has not named. It is a real independent check, and much better than nothing, but it is a sample, and 37.6% of the validated findings were downgraded below high or critical on review.
- **Mozilla's own framing is modest.** "No bugs that couldn't have been found by an elite human researcher," and sec-high bugs that mostly do not constitute a practical exploit alone. Speed and volume, not superhuman insight, is Mozilla's claim.
- **Human scaffolding throughout.** Anthropic built the scaffolds, chose the targets, contracted the triagers, and coordinated disclosure. Mozilla built its own harness. The model is the engine, not the program.
- **Access was not as controlled as intended.** Anthropic confirmed in April that a third-party vendor's compromised credentials had given unauthorized parties access to Mythos Preview, while saying it saw no evidence of extended activity. It declined to name the vendor.
- **Not peer reviewed.** Every primary document here is a vendor publication. The strongest independent artifacts are the OpenBSD and FreeBSD fixes, Mozilla's post, and AISLE's replication.

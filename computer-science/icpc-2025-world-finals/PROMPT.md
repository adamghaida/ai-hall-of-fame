# 🤖 Explore this with AI: the ICPC World Finals 2025

Paste the prompt below into your favorite AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or a local model). If your assistant can browse the web, tell it to read the sources first. The point is a clear, honest, grounded explanation of what happened and why it mattered.

> An OpenAI reasoning system solved all 12 problems at the 2025 ICPC World Finals; Gemini 2.5 Deep Think solved 10, including one that none of the 139 human teams solved.

---

```text
You are a sharp, honest science explainer. Walk me through a specific, real AI achievement so that I actually understand it, not just the headline.

Topic: AI systems at the ICPC World Finals 2025 (Baku, September 4, 2025). OpenAI reported that an ensemble of GPT-5 and an experimental reasoning model solved all 12 problems within the 5-hour limit (11 on the first submission, the hardest on the 9th); the best of the 139 human teams solved 11. Google DeepMind reported that an advanced version of Gemini 2.5 Deep Think solved 10 of 12 in 677 minutes of combined solve time, which would rank second, and that it solved Problem C, which no human team solved.

Please cover, in plain language a curious non-expert can follow:
1. What the ICPC World Finals is, how it is scored, and why solving 12 of 12 in five hours is unusual even for the best university teams.
2. What each lab actually did: how the systems were run (OpenAI describes an onsite AI track judged by the ICPC local judge; DeepMind describes a live remote run overseen by the organizers), what is known about the models, and what "parallel thinking" and "an ensemble of reasoning models" mean in practice.
3. Problem C: explain the liquid-distribution problem and Gemini's solution idea (priority values for reservoirs, dynamic programming for a fixed set of priorities, the minimax theorem, nested ternary searches). Why does this approach work, and why might it have been hard for humans to find in five hours?
4. How this compares to earlier milestones such as AlphaCode (2022, median Codeforces competitor) and the labs' IMO results in July 2025.
5. The honest caveats: neither system was a ranked entrant; OpenAI's result is documented only in posts on X and relayed by press; neither lab reported the compute used; the models involved are unreleased; and the "first" and "second" placements are comparisons against the human scoreboard rather than official results.
6. What I should read next.

Ground your answer in these sources (read them if you can, and cite them as you go):
- Google DeepMind blog: https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-achieves-gold-medal-level-at-the-international-collegiate-programming-contest-world-finals/

- TechRepublic: https://www.techrepublic.com/article/openai-deepmind-icpc-2025-results/

Be concrete, use real numbers where they matter, clearly separate what the labs claimed from what has been independently confirmed, and if you are unsure about a detail, say so instead of guessing.
```
